# 05 — Competitive Game Analysis

## Purpose

The core evidence base of the lab. Here you conduct deep, honest analysis of your own serious games, first independently and then with engine and mentor input, to expose the real reasons for results.

## What to Store

- PGN files of all serious games (classical and rapid), each with an analysis file.
- Completed game analysis templates.
- Notes captured at the board, such as time spent and key thoughts, where possible.
- Critical-position collections extracted from your games for later training (link to `03`).
- Cross-game pattern notes.
- Analysis of relevant model games by stronger players for comparison.

## Recommended Workflow

1. Save the PGN and your own notes immediately after each game while memory is fresh.
2. Analyze without an engine first. Record your candidate moves, evaluations, and the thoughts you actually had at critical moments.
3. Then check with an engine and, where possible, a stronger player (see `06`); record where your assessment differed.
4. Classify errors (calculation, evaluation, strategy, opening, endgame, time, psychology).
5. Extract lessons and 1-3 concrete positions or themes for training; send patterns to `08`.

## Expected Outputs

- Complete analysis for every serious game.
- A tagged error database that supports pattern detection.
- A steady flow of critical positions into training.

## Review Process

- Ask whether the analysis explains *why* the errors happened, not just what the best move was.
- Calculate error frequency by category monthly; identify the largest recurring categories.
- Compare unaided analysis with engine verdicts to measure your evaluation accuracy.
- Check that lessons resulted in changes in training (`02`).

## Suggested Subfolders or Templates

Recommended: `games/YYYY/`, `analysis/YYYY/`, `critical-positions/`, `model-games/`, `error-database/`. Included template: `game-analysis-template.md`.
