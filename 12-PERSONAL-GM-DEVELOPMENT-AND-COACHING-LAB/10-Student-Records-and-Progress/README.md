# 10 — Student Records and Progress

## Purpose

Maintains accurate, respectful, and useful records of students you teach, so that instruction is individualized and progress is measured by evidence. Demonstrates your coaching effectiveness over time.

## What to Store

- One file or subfolder per student: profile, goals, baseline assessment, lesson history, homework, and results.
- Progress reports (from the template) at regular intervals.
- Assessment results: tests, game results, and rating changes where available.
- Communication records that are relevant to teaching (schedule, agreements).
- Consent and safeguarding records where required.
- Anonymized or aggregate summaries used for your own analysis.

## Recommended Workflow

1. Open a record for each new student: goals, level, learning preferences, baseline test.
2. After each lesson, add a brief note: topics, understanding shown, homework, and next step.
3. Write a progress report at agreed intervals (e.g., every 8-12 lessons or each term) and share it with the student or guardian.
4. Compare against baseline; adjust the plan when data show no progress.
5. Archive inactive students according to your retention policy.

## Expected Outputs

- Up-to-date records for every student.
- Periodic progress reports with evidence.
- An aggregate view of what teaching approaches work.

## Review Process

- Ask whether each student's improvement is documented by data, not only by your impression.
- Look for students who are stagnating and investigate the cause and the method.
- Compare outcomes across students to refine your methods; use anonymized data.
- Review privacy compliance regularly.

## Suggested Subfolders or Templates

Recommended: `_template/`, one subfolder per student named `Lastname-Firstname` or an ID code, `_archive/`. Included template: `student-progress-report-template.md`. **Privacy note:** store only necessary information, obtain consent (and guardian consent for minors), follow local data-protection and safeguarding rules, and consider using ID codes instead of names.
