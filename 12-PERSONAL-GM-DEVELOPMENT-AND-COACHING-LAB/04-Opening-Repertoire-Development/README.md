# 04 — Opening Repertoire Development

## Purpose

Builds a repertoire that suits your style and is tested against real results, not only theory. Openings here are treated as working hypotheses about which positions you play well, which are then verified in games.

## What to Store

- Repertoire files for White and for Black (main lines, sidelines, and responses to unusual moves).
- Prepared lines with your own annotations: plans, key ideas, typical pawn structures, and traps.
- Repertoire maps or trees (PGN or database references).
- Opponent preparation notes.
- Results statistics by opening from your own games.
- Records of opening mistakes with the correct alternatives.

## Recommended Workflow

1. Choose repertoire elements by style fit, structural understanding, and long-term maintainability.
2. Study model games and typical structures before memorizing lines.
3. After each tournament, compare your actual games with your preparation; record deviations and errors.
4. Update files using the opening line review template.
5. Every quarter, run a repertoire audit using your results and engine-checked critical lines.

## Expected Outputs

- A maintained, annotated repertoire for both colors.
- An opening statistics table from your real games.
- A list of lines to add, repair, or drop.

## Review Process

- Look at the score and average move quality by opening, not just win-loss.
- Identify whether losses came from theory gaps, misunderstanding of plans, or time use.
- Check the balance between memorized preparation and understanding; can you explain the main plans without notes?
- Avoid changing repertoire after small samples; require sufficient evidence.

## Suggested Subfolders or Templates

Recommended: `white/`, `black-vs-e4/`, `black-vs-d4/`, `black-vs-other/`, `opponent-prep/`, `statistics/`. Included template: `opening-line-review-template.md`.
