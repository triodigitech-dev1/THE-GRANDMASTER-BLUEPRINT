# Content Standards: The Grandmaster Blueprint

> This document defines the quality, accuracy, and compliance standards for all content produced within _The Grandmaster Blueprint_ curriculum.
> All standards are derived from and aligned with **FIDE (International Chess Federation)** official regulations, handbooks, and commissions.
> Last aligned with FIDE regulations effective **2026**.

---

## 1. Governing Principle

All chess content in this curriculum must be **FIDE-compliant**, **factually accurate**, **pedagogically sound**, and **verifiably sourced**.

No content may contradict or supersede FIDE regulations. Where FIDE regulations change, curriculum content must be updated to match.

---

## 2. Source Hierarchy

Content authors must source from the following in order of precedence:

| Priority | Source                                           | Authority                                    |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| **1**    | FIDE Handbook (handbook.fide.com)                | Official regulations                         |
| **2**    | FIDE Commission publications (RCC, ARB, TRG, QC) | Interpretations & guidance                   |
| **3**    | FIDE Arbiters' Manual (current edition)          | Practical application of laws                |
| **4**    | FIDE Trainers' Commission (TRG) Guides           | Trainer standards                            |
| **5**    | National federation regulations                  | Local adaptations (must not contradict FIDE) |
| **6**    | Peer-reviewed chess literature                   | Supplementary theory only                    |

> **Rule:** If a conflict exists between a lower-priority source and a higher-priority source, the higher-priority source prevails. The content must be corrected.

## 3. Rules & Laws of Chess Standards

### 3.1 Official Rule Source

All rules content must reference the **FIDE Laws of Chess** as published in the FIDE Handbook and maintained by the **Rules Commission (RCC)**[reference:0].

### 3.2 Version Control

- Content must state the **effective date** of the Laws of Chess used.
- Where rules have changed, the content must reflect the **current version** and note the change if historically relevant.
- The FIDE Handbook is the authoritative repository for all current regulations[reference:1].

### 3.3 Scope of Rules Coverage

Content must correctly distinguish between:

- **Basic Rules of Play** (Article 1–5)
- **Competition Rules** (Article 6–12)
- **Appendices** (rapid, blitz, algebraic notation, etc.)

### 3.4 Practical Application

Where rules are explained, content must include:

- The **exact article reference** (e.g., Article 7.5.1).
- A **practical scenario** demonstrating correct application.
- A **common misconception** warning where relevant.

> The FIDE Arbiters' Manual 2026 explicitly notes that "knowing the rules is one thing but understanding how to apply them correctly in real situations is another"[reference:2]. Content must bridge this gap.

## 4. Notation & Game Recording Standards

### 4.1 Algebraic Notation

All notation content must comply with **FIDE Appendix C – Algebraic Notation**.

| Element       | Standard                                   |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Piece letters | K, Q, R, B, N (English)                    |
| Pawn moves    | No letter prefix (e.g., e4, d5)            |
| Captures      | `x` (e.g., Nxf3)                           |
| Check         | `+`                                        |
| Checkmate     | `#`                                        |
| Castling      | `O-O` (kingside), `O-O-O` (queenside)      |
| Promotion     | `=Q` (e.g., e8=Q)                          |
| En passant    | `exd6 e.p.` (or standard capture notation) |

### 4.2 PGN (Portable Game Notation)

- All PGN content must conform to the **PGN Standard** specification.
- Seven Tag Roster (STR) must be complete and correctly ordered.
- Move text must be valid and parseable by standard chess software (Lichess, ChessBase, SCID).

### 4.3 Scoresheet Standards

- All scoresheet templates must comply with **FIDE Competition Rules** for game recording.
- Must include: Event, Date, White, Black, Result, and move number columns.
- Must be usable in FIDE-rated tournaments.

### 4.4 Digital Recording

- Content covering electronic scoresheets must reference **FIDE Handbook C.02** (Chess Equipment Technical Specifications)[reference:3].
- Must note that electronic recording devices must be FIDE-approved for FIDE-rated events.

## 5. Rating & Title Standards

### 5.1 FIDE Rating Regulations

All rating content must align with the **FIDE Rating Regulations** maintained by the **Qualification Commission (QC)**.

| Element                          | Standard                            |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Rating system                    | Elo                                 |
| Minimum games for initial rating | Per current FIDE Rating Regulations |
| Rating floors                    | Per current FIDE Rating Regulations |
| K-factor                         | Per current FIDE Rating Regulations |
| Time control eligibility         | Standard, Rapid, Blitz as defined   |

### 5.2 Time Control Standards (2026 Update)

Effective for the 2026 calendar:

- Tournaments with a time control of **45 minutes + 30 seconds increment (45+30)** or longer (e.g., 60+30) **may be rated as standard chess**, subject to QC approval[reference:4].
- This applies only to **major or traditional events** supported by a national or continental federation[reference:5].
- **Norm limitations:** No more than **one such norm** may be counted per title[reference:6].

### 5.3 Title Regulations

All title content must align with **FIDE Title Regulations** (effective from 1 January 2022, as amended)[reference:7].

#### 5.3.1 Recognized Titles

| Title                      | Abbreviation |
| -------------------------- | ------------ |
| Grandmaster                | GM           |
| International Master       | IM           |
| FIDE Master                | FM           |
| Candidate Master           | CM           |
| Woman Grandmaster          | WGM          |
| Woman International Master | WIM          |
| Woman FIDE Master          | WFM          |
| Woman Candidate Master     | WCM          |

> Titles are valid for life from the date confirmed[reference:8].

#### 5.3.2 Title Performance Standards

| Title | Performance Rating | Opponent Average Rating |
| ----- | ------------------ | ----------------------- |
| GM    | ≥ 2600             | ≥ 2380                  |
| IM    | ≥ 2450             | ≥ 2230                  |
| WGM   | ≥ 2400             | ≥ 2180                  |
| WIM   | ≥ 2250             | ≥ 2030                  |

_Source: FIDE Title Regulations 0.5_[reference:9].

#### 5.3.3 Direct Title Rating Floors

For a direct title to be awarded immediately, the applicant must have achieved at some time a minimum rating:

| Title | Minimum Rating |
| ----- | -------------- |
| GM    | 2300           |
| WGM   | 2100           |
| IM    | 2200           |
| WIM   | 2000           |
| FM    | 2100           |
| WFM   | 1900           |
| CM    | 2000           |
| WCM   | 1800           |

_Source: FIDE Title Regulations 0.62_[reference:10].

#### 5.3.4 Norm Requirements

| Title | Norms Required | Key Conditions                              |
| ----- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| GM    | 3 norms        | ≥ 1/3 opponents must be GMs (min. 3)        |
| IM    | 3 norms        | ≥ 1/3 opponents must be IMs or GMs (min. 3) |
| WGM   | 3 norms        | Per regulations                             |
| WIM   | 3 norms        | Per regulations                             |

_Source: FIDE Title Regulations_[reference:11].

#### 5.3.5 Ethics & Revocation

- Use of a FIDE title or rating to subvert ethical principles may result in **revocation**[reference:12].
- Breach of Fair Play Regulations during title-qualifying tournaments may result in **title removal**[reference:13].

## 6. Arbiter & Organizer Standards

### 6.1 Arbiter Titles

All arbiter content must align with **FIDE Regulations for the Titles of Arbiters** (B.06.1, effective 1 March 2026)[reference:14].

| Title                 | Abbreviation | Level                           |
| --------------------- | ------------ | ------------------------------- |
| FIDE Arbiter          | FA           | Entry-level international title |
| International Arbiter | IA           | Advanced international title    |

_Source: B.06.1, Article 1.1_[reference:15].

### 6.2 Arbiter Norms

| Title | Norms Required |
| ----- | -------------- |
| FA    | 4 norms        |
| IA    | 5 norms        |

_Source: B.06.1, Article 2.2_[reference:16].

### 6.3 Seminar Requirement

- Attendance of **one FIDE Arbiters' Seminar**.
- Successful passing (≥ 80%) of an examination set by the Arbiters' Commission[reference:17].

### 6.4 Event Types Acceptable for Norms

- FIDE Rated Events (Swiss, Round Robin, Double Round Robin, Team, Knockout)
- Hybrid chess FIDE-rated events (pre-endorsed)
- Seminars[reference:18]

### 6.5 Organizer Standards

All organizer content must align with:

- **FIDE Handbook B.09** – Regulations on Seminars & Title Award for Organizers.
- **FIDE Handbook C.04** – Swiss Rules and pairing systems[reference:19].
- **FIDE Handbook C.07** – Play-Off and Tie-Break Regulations[reference:20].

### 6.6 Tournament Equipment

- All equipment referenced must comply with **FIDE Handbook C.02** – Chess Equipment Technical Specifications[reference:21].
- Digital clocks must meet FIDE technical requirements (e.g., accuracy ±1 second per hour, visible at 10 meters, etc.)[reference:22].

## 7. Trainer & Coach Standards

### 7.1 Trainer Titles

All coaching content must align with **FIDE Trainers' Commission (TRG)** regulations[reference:23].

| Title                    | Abbreviation | Rating Requirement | Scope                    |
| ------------------------ | ------------ | ------------------ | ------------------------ |
| FIDE Senior Trainer      | FST          | 2450 (career top)  | Train players above 2450 |
| FIDE Trainer             | FT           | 2200               | Train players up to 2450 |
| FIDE Instructor          | FI           | 2000               | Train players up to 2000 |
| National Instructor      | NI           | 1800               | Train players up to 1800 |
| Developmental Instructor | DI           | 1600               | Simple instructions      |

_Source: TRG Guide, Annex 58_[reference:24][reference:25].

### 7.2 Trainer Licensing

- A titled Trainer is charged a **Licence Fee** after two calendar years.
- Each licence is valid for **four (4) years**[reference:26].

### 7.3 Trainer Seminar Standards

- Content covering trainer certification must reference **TRG Seminars** and FIDE Academies[reference:27].
- FIDE Senior Trainer applications require published material and proof of international successes[reference:28].

---

## 8. Fair Play & Ethics Standards

### 8.1 Anti-Cheating

- All content must align with **FIDE Fair Play Regulations**.
- Content must address: electronic device policies, anti-cheating devices, and fair play in hybrid/online events[reference:29].

### 8.2 Ethics

- Content must reference **FIDE Ethics Commission** regulations (Handbook A.08).
- Any discussion of titles or ratings must include the ethical revocation clause[reference:30].

### 8.3 Hybrid & Online Chess

- Content covering online/hybrid events must reference **FIDE Online Arena Regulations** and hybrid chess endorsements[reference:31].

---

## 9. Accessibility & Inclusivity Standards

### 9.1 Language

- All content must be produced in **English** as the primary language.
- Where translations are produced, the English version remains authoritative.

### 9.2 Format Accessibility

- All written content must be available in accessible formats (screen-reader compatible, high contrast).
- Diagrams must include alternative text descriptions.
- Notation must be provided in both algebraic and descriptive forms where pedagogically useful.

### 9.3 Inclusive Representation

- Historical content must represent players across genders, regions, and eras.
- Profiles and examples must include women players, players from developing chess nations, and players with disabilities where relevant.

---

## 10. Content Production Workflow

### 10.1 Authoring

1. Identify the relevant FIDE regulation.
2. Draft content with direct citations.
3. Cross-check against the FIDE Handbook.
4. Validate all notation and PGN with software.

### 10.2 Review

| Review Stage          | Reviewer                          |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Technical accuracy    | Certified FIDE Arbiter or Trainer |
| Pedagogical soundness | Curriculum designer               |
| Compliance            | FIDE regulation checklist         |
| Accessibility         | Accessibility reviewer            |

### 10.3 Version Control

- Every content file must include:
  - **Version number**
  - **Date of last FIDE regulation alignment**
  - **List of FIDE sources referenced**

### 10.4 Update Triggers

Content must be reviewed and updated when:

- FIDE amends the Laws of Chess.
- FIDE amends Rating or Title Regulations.
- FIDE amends Arbiter or Trainer regulations.
- New FIDE Handbooks or Manuals are published.

---

## 11. Compliance Checklist for Every Content File

Before publication, each content file must satisfy:

- [ ] All rules statements cite the FIDE Laws of Chess.
- [ ] All notation follows FIDE Appendix C.
- [ ] All rating statements cite FIDE Rating Regulations.
- [ ] All title statements cite FIDE Title Regulations.
- [ ] All arbiter statements cite B.06.1 (or successor).
- [ ] All trainer statements cite TRG regulations.
- [ ] All equipment statements cite FIDE Handbook C.02.
- [ ] Ethical and Fair Play standards are referenced.
- [ ] Accessibility requirements are met.
- [ ] Version and alignment date are recorded.
